How we check information

Where the facts on xpat4.org come from, what the dates and labels mean, and what to do if you find a mistake.

Published November 20, 2026. Checked November 20, 2026.
Report a mistake

Where facts come from

We only write what we have read on an open page: an official text of a law or resolution, a government or embassy website, a major Kyrgyz news outlet. A search-engine summary is not a source. We do not use websites that sell their own services as a source of figures.

Amounts, deadlines and fines

We publish fines, ban periods, tax rates and prices only if they appear in an official text or in two independent sources. We check fines and tax rates against the Code of Offences and the Tax Code in the Ministry of Justice database. If a figure is missing or sources disagree, we say so.

Dates and labels

  • Every guide shows a check date: the day its facts were checked against sources.
  • The “What changed” block shows what was edited and when.
  • News items carry labels: “In force”, “Coming soon”, “Draft only”. A draft is not yet a law.
  • We check for news every day. If there is nothing new and reliable, we publish nothing.

Older articles

Some articles were written before these rules. They carry a yellow note at the top. For verified answers by situation, see the guides.

What we do not do

  • We do not publish invented stories, reviews or quotes.
  • We do not draw legal conclusions that are not in the source.
  • We run no ads. The “Useful service” block is our recommendation, not a paid placement.

Found a mistake

Every guide page has a “Report a mistake” button: it opens WhatsApp with the page title. We will fix the text and record the edit in the “What changed” block.

xpat4.org does not replace a consultation. If your situation differs from what is described, describe it in 3 steps and Gulnara Mambetaliyeva, a migration consultant, will reply personally.
