Chaldybar border crossing with Kazakhstan closes to vehicles from 25 November

From 01:00 on 25 November the Chaldybar checkpoint in Chuy region stops letting through cars, trucks and buses, in both directions. You can still cross on foot as usual. The reason is major construction at the Kazakh checkpoint Sypatay Batyr on the other side. The Kazakh side's preliminary estimate is that vehicle traffic will reopen by the end of 2027. The Border Service suggests the Ak-Zhol, Ak-Tilek, Ken-Bulun or Tokmok checkpoints instead. The reports don't say which vehicle types each of them accepts, so check before you drive.

Lawmakers want to ban surrogacy for foreigners in Kyrgyzstan

An interagency commission is drafting a bill that would make surrogacy in Kyrgyzstan available only to Kyrgyz citizens. Foreigners and stateless people would be barred. The current law has no such ban. Two drafts went out for discussion in August, one from MP Bolot Ibragimov and one from Speaker Marlen Mamataliev. The Speaker's draft cites Health Ministry figures: 153 children born to surrogate mothers were registered in 2024 and 2025, 138 of them to foreigners. The Health Ministry, though, told the outlet it keeps no central statistics split by citizens and foreigners. This is still a draft: parliament has not passed it, and a single version is still being worked out.
Source: 24.kg

Foreigners are turning to Kyrgyzstan's ombudsman 2.5 times more often

From January to October 2026 the Akyikatchy (Ombudsman) Institute received 91 complaints from foreigners from 21 countries. In the whole of 2025 there were 36, from 11 countries. Most came from citizens of Pakistan (27), Russia (23), Turkey (6) and Uzbekistan (5). The ombudsman gave the figures at an international conference. Foreigners in Kyrgyzstan have the right to complain to the ombudsman about state bodies and officials. You can file in writing, through the online form at ombudsman.kg, or on the free 115 hotline. We explain how in a separate article.
Source: Economist.kg

Kyrgyzstan proposes a 60-day visa-free stay and 18 more visa-free countries

Kyrgyzstan's State Agency for Tourism Development has put a draft Cabinet resolution out for public discussion. It would extend the allowed visa-free stay from 30 days within any 60 days to 60 days within any 120 days, for every country already on the main visa-free list, not just new ones. Separately, the draft would add 18 countries to that list: Indonesia, the Philippines, Jordan, Morocco, South Africa, Venezuela, Nepal, Tunisia, Colombia, the Dominican Republic, Costa Rica, Ecuador, Peru, Panama, the Seychelles, Guatemala, Trinidad and Tobago, and Jamaica. The agency says the longer stay is mainly meant for high-altitude tourism: climbing expeditions to Lenin Peak, Khan Tengri and Victory Peak take 45 to 60 days. This is still a draft — it only takes effect once the Cabinet adopts the resolution, and registration and border-control rules stay in place either way.
Source: Economist.kg

Tax service publishes 2027 reference salaries — they set the tax floor if you're paid less

On October 30, 2026, the Tax Service published the 2027 reference average monthly salary (SMZ) by region — the figure used, in cases set by law, to calculate income tax and insurance contributions when an employee's official salary is lower. The nationwide figure rose from 36,047 to 42,493 som; in Bishkek, from 44,074 to 54,354 som. There's also a lower version that excludes the region's highest-paying companies — 33,727 som for Bishkek. Which figure applies depends on the employer's category and contribution rate. This affects every employer in Kyrgyzstan, including foreign-owned companies and sole proprietors with staff, if the salary in the employment contract is below the reference figure. The SMZ is not a mandatory minimum wage, just a tax calculation base; the new figures apply from January 1, 2027.
Source: Economist.kg

New company registration and liquidation rules let foreigners use another ID number instead of a Kyrgyz PIN

Cabinet Resolution No. 746, signed November 9, 2026, changes the rules for registering, re-registering and shutting down legal entities, branches and representative offices in Kyrgyzstan. Foreign citizens filing company paperwork can now give a different ID number instead of a Kyrgyz personal ID number (PIN). A re-registration decision must now spell out every beneficial owner's full name, citizenship, ID number and address. When a company is liquidated, the registering authority will check through inter-agency data exchange for unpaid taxes, insurance contributions, non-tax payments and customs debts — if there is a debt, liquidation is refused. The resolution was published November 10 and takes effect November 20, 2026. If you own or plan to register a company in Kyrgyzstan, the new beneficiary-disclosure and debt-check rules apply from that date.
Source: Economist.kg

MPs propose a warning instead of a fine for foreigners who cover their face in public

Kyrgyzstan's parliament has put out for public discussion a bill on fines for fully covering one's face in government buildings and public places. The general fine would drop from 20,000 to 10,000 som. For foreign citizens specifically, the bill proposes something different: a warning for a first violation, and only a 10,000-som fine if it happens again within a year. Foreigners would still have to uncover their face temporarily when needed for an ID check. The bill's authors say Kyrgyzstan draws tourists from countries where face-covering clothing is part of their tradition, and they don't want a fine to sour a first-time visitor's trip. This is only a draft so far, not an adopted law.
Source: 24.kg

Draft only

MPs draft a law on land under property bought by foreigners — up to 50 years of temporary use

A group of MPs has put forward a bill spelling out what happens to the land plot when a foreigner buys a house, flat or non-residential space. The plot under the property would transfer, free of charge, to the state or a municipality, and the buyer would get a right of temporary use of the land — 20 years at first, extendable by another 20, then 10 more, up to 50 years total. For land already tied to property owned by foreigners, the bill proposes a 12-month inventory, then six months for owners to hand the plot to the state voluntarily; if they refuse, a court would decide. The bill doesn't ban foreigners from buying property — it only changes how the land under it is registered, since the Constitution already bars foreigners from owning land outright. This is still a draft, not an adopted law.

In force

Currency exchange offices now ID you for any amount, big or small

Since October 16, 2026, currency exchange offices in Kyrgyzstan must verify a customer's identity for every cash foreign-currency transaction, regardless of the amount. A passport isn't the only option — a Kyrgyz residence permit, driver's license or military ID also work. For deals of 100,000 som and up, the office logs the customer's details and the transaction; for 1 million som and up, the check goes deeper, including who actually owns the money. The National Bank says each exchange office stores this data itself for at least five years and is responsible for keeping it safe; customers can ask what data about them is held. If you exchange currency in Kyrgyzstan, bring your passport or residence permit even for a small amount.
Source: Economist.kg

Kazakhstan phases in its QazETA entry permit — not mandatory yet

Starting November 1, 2026, Kazakhstan began phasing in QazETA, its electronic entry permit: the first stage covers Aktau airport and the Urlyutobe, Aukhatty, Atameken and Nurly Zhol border crossings. The rule applies to foreigners entering visa-free, including Kyrgyzstani citizens. Until July 1, 2027, it's only recommended — not having a QazETA isn't by itself grounds for refusal. You apply through the QazETA app up to 72 hours before travel; it's free and valid for 180 days. Astana and Almaty airports join on January 1, 2027, the remaining crossings on March 1, and it becomes mandatory on July 1. If you're planning a trip to Kazakhstan, getting a QazETA in advance doesn't hurt, even though it isn't required yet.
Source: Economist.kg

The fee rule for foreign companies' bank-account accreditation is now in force — the amount isn't set yet

As of November 1, 2026, provisions of Law No. 145 of August 6 are in force: non-resident companies that want to open or keep an account at a Kyrgyz bank will have to pay a fee for a permit or accreditation. The Cabinet of Ministers still has to set the fee amount and payment procedure in a separate resolution. The Ministry of Economy earlier proposed 200,000 som, but that draft is still under discussion, and the law taking effect doesn't mean the amount is approved. Under the draft, the accreditation and registry would be run by the state agency Kyrgyz Export. If your non-resident company uses accounts at Kyrgyz banks, watch for the resolution on the fee amount separately from the law provision already in force.
Source: Economist.kg

Draft only

Economy Ministry proposes a 200,000-som accreditation fee for foreign companies' bank accounts

Kyrgyzstan's Ministry of Economy has put out for public discussion a draft rule: foreign companies that want to open or keep a bank account in Kyrgyzstan would need accreditation and a 200,000-som fee. The fee is paid before submitting documents, and half of it goes to the regulator's development fund. The process: the company files with a bank, which checks the company and its beneficiaries, then forwards the file to the state body 'Kyrgyz Export' under the Ministry of Economy, which keeps the registry and issues an accreditation certificate within five working days. The certificate doesn't obligate the bank to open an account — each bank still decides on non-resident clients on its own, and if accreditation is refused, the fee is generally not returned. The draft hasn't been adopted yet and its terms could change — if your non-resident company uses accounts at Kyrgyz banks, watch for the final text of the resolution.
Source: Economist.kg

Overstayers can get an exit visa right at Manas airport — for triple the price

The Consular Department of the Foreign Ministry clarified that citizens of 67 countries under the simplified stay regime who overstayed can get an exit visa right at Manas airport before their flight. This express service costs three times the normal fee. For citizens of other countries, the clarification does not cover getting a visa at the airport — they need to sort it out in advance. You can apply for an exit visa ahead of time, without the markup, through the e-Visa portal. The Foreign Ministry did not give the exact fee amount, so check it on the portal or with the Consular Department before you travel.

Kyrgyzstan caps coal prices for households for 90 days

Since October 22, 2026, Kyrgyzstan has capped retail coal prices for households for 90 days, with the option to extend. The rule comes from Order No. 219 of the Ministry of Economy and Commerce. The Anti-Monopoly Service sets a separate ceiling price for each region plus Bishkek and Osh; when the order was issued, Bishkek was selling Kara-Kече coal at 7,000 som a ton and Shabyrkul at 7,800. Sellers who charge more face fines: 3,000 som for an individual, 13,000 for a company. If you heat with coal, ask for a receipt and check the price tag matches what you're quoted — inspectors check for that too.
Source: Economist.kg

October's 'Illegal' raids: 1,487 citations against foreigners and their employers

GKNB and the police reported the results of the October 1–10 'Illegal' raids: 1,487 administrative citations totaling 13.4 million soms. Most of them — 1,412 — were issued under Article 431 of the Code on Offenses, covering violations of foreigners' stay and work rules; fines there came to 10.48 million soms. Another 75 citations worth 2.93 million soms went to employers under Article 93, for breaking the rules on hiring foreign labor. Officials also ran outreach with foreigners and the companies that employ them, though the report gives no details on that. If you work in Kyrgyzstan without an official permit, or employ foreigners without the right paperwork, the checks are ongoing, and both the worker and the employer can be fined.
Source: Economist.kg

Kyrgyzstan keeps its 2027 permanent-residence quota at 400 people

The Cabinet of Ministers' order No. 780-т of October 13 keeps the 2027 immigration quota at 400 people, unchanged from 2026. This quota covers only immigrant status — permanent residence — and does not limit labor migrants or tourists. The regional breakdown stays the same: Chuy region gets 110 slots, Bishkek 100, Jalal-Abad and Osh regions 50 each, Osh city 30, Issyk-Kul and Naryn regions 20 each, and Batken and Talas 10 each. Priority goes to ethnic Kyrgyz, certain categories of relatives of Kyrgyz citizens, and highly qualified specialists the economy needs, and applications take up to nine months to review. If you are planning permanent residence in Kyrgyzstan, the 2027 cap and priority rules are the same as in 2026.
Source: Economist.kg

Bishkek keeps raiding couriers and taxi drivers who are foreigners without a work permit

Bishkek's city police reported on October 13 another round of "Illegal" and "Moto-courier" raids: more than 30 foreign nationals were brought in for checks. Officers verified the legality of people's stay, their documents and work permits among those working as couriers and drivers. The raids produced 15 administrative citations against foreigners, with fines topping 200,000 som; police are separately checking whether taxi companies hired foreigners in violation of the rules. Some of those detained were students riding mopeds for their own errands rather than working as couriers, and they were released after a warning talk. Police said raids to identify migration-law violations are continuing. If you work or plan to work in Kyrgyzstan as a courier or taxi driver, you need an official work permit — without one, a check can mean a fine.

Buy a house in Kyrgyzstan as a foreigner, and the land under it comes as temporary use only

The head of the State Agency for Land Resources, Ernisbek Zarnaev, confirmed at an October 7 session of parliament: when a foreigner buys a house or building in Kyrgyzstan, the land under it is registered not as ownership but as temporary use, for up to 50 years. The reason is that the Constitution bars foreigners from owning land, which leaves the plot's owner formally undefined. If the foreigner later sells the house to a Kyrgyz citizen, the land automatically becomes that buyer's private property. MP Dastan Bekeshev called the land's status unclear and said a group of deputies is drafting a bill for the voluntary transfer of such plots to the state; deputy prime minister Daniyar Amangeldiev said he was open to discussing the idea separately. The amendments have not been adopted, so the current rule still applies: a foreigner can own the house, but only holds temporary use of the land beneath it.
Source: 24.kg

Starlink's pilot period in Kyrgyzstan has ended, and its legal status is still unclear

Kyrgyzstan's year-long pilot period for Starlink satellite internet ended on October 5 — it had run since October 6, 2025, under presidential decree No. 276. The service itself has not stopped: Starlink's Kyrgyzstan website keeps taking orders and listing prices in som. On the same day, October 5, the Cabinet approved new rules for licensing telecom operators and radio-frequency fees, but neither Starlink nor SpaceX is mentioned in those documents. As of October 6, the authorities had published neither a final report on the pilot nor any document spelling out what legal regime satellite internet now falls under. If you use Starlink or are thinking of signing up, know that the service is available, but its status after October 5 has not been officially clarified.
Source: Economist.kg

Residence permits and foreigner registration move to the Labor Ministry

The president signed a decree: the Ministry of Labor, Social Security and Migration will take over issuing residence permits, registering foreigners, and handling the unified permit and the resident card. Until now this was done by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and Kyzmat. The ministry will set up a migration department and plans to outsource the services themselves. It is not yet said when the transition starts or where to apply: the government has two months to bring its own decisions in line with the decree. Until something else is announced, go to the office you used before.

New registration rules for foreigners are in force

A new procedure for registering foreigners has been in force since September 23. You register after you enter the country, and no later than the last day you may stay without registration. For citizens of the USA, the UK, Canada, Japan, South Korea and a number of European countries this is 30 days. If you leave Kyrgyzstan for 30 days or more, the registration stops being valid and you register again after you return. If you change address or passport, you have to re-register within 10 working days. For a flat or house the rule is at least 12 m² of living space per person, and it does not apply to close relatives. More for tenants.

Overstayed? You can be refused visa-free entry next time

Resolution No. 602 of September 7: a foreigner who stayed longer than allowed will be refused entry under the visa-free regime next time. The length of stay is now counted by an automated system (EISUM). Spouses and minor children of Kyrgyz citizens can enter once with documents proving the relationship, and they get 30 working days to sort out their status. The article does not say for how long entry is closed. The resolution takes effect 10 days after publication, which was on September 8. What to do before and after an overstay.
